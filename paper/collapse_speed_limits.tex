\title{No-Signaling Speed Limits on Continuous Wavefunction Collapse:\\
A Universal Log-Tail Bound and Mixed-State Finite-Time Escape Hatches}

\author{Nicolas Adamini}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Continuous objective collapse models such as Continuous Spontaneous Localization (CSL) prevent operational superluminal signaling by ensuring that ensemble averages obey a linear Lindblad master equation, yet suffer from the ``tails problem'': losing branches retain strictly nonzero amplitudes for all finite times. We establish a $d$-level speed limit (attained for $d=2$ and for single-projector dephasing) governing this obstruction in pure-dephasing measurement dynamics ($[H,P_k]=0$, excluding CSL with a non-commuting kinetic term). For any Markovian continuous It\^o diffusion on pure states $\mathbb{C}P^{d-1}$ unraveling a Born-preserving Lindblad master equation with finite integrated dephasing, no initially populated branch can reach zero in finite time, and every branch obeys a universal log-tail lower bound. For $d=2$, homodyne CSL saturates the pointwise volatility bound and globally minimizes $\E[\ln p_t]$ among all Markovian pure-state unravelings. Keeping Born conservation fixed, we exhibit four sufficient no-signaling escape hatches relaxing continuity, finite integrated dephasing, Markovianity (via a unique Erlang-2 randomized Brody--Hughston mixture), and trajectory purity (via a Markovian continuous mixed-state SDE on density matrices $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^d \otimes \mathcal{H}_B)$ with an exact closed-form mean collapse time). We cross-check all analytical results with a reproducible standalone Monte Carlo script and detail the scope limits of the mixed-state construction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The measurement (or ``one-outcome'') problem in quantum mechanics asks how a superposition $\ket{\psi_0} = \sum_{j=1}^d \sqrt{p_j(0)} e^{i\phi_j(0)} \ket{j}$ resolves into a single definite outcome $\ket{k^*}$ with Born probability $p_{k^*}(0)$. Dynamical reduction models---most notably Ghirardi--Rimini--Weber (GRW) jump models~\cite{GRW1986} and Continuous Spontaneous Localization (CSL)~\cite{Pearle1989,GPR1990,BassiGhirardi2003,Bassi2013}---modify Schr\"odinger evolution by adding stochastic nonlinear terms that localize macroscopic superpositions while maintaining Born statistics and operational no-signaling~\cite{Gisin1984,Gisin1989,HJW1993}.

A longstanding critique of continuous collapse models is the \emph{tails problem}~\cite{BassiGhirardi2003}: while losing amplitudes decay exponentially fast for practical purposes, they never strictly vanish at any finite time $t < \infty$. Diffusive pure-state unravelings of Markovian Lindblad master equations have been completely parameterized by Wiseman and Di\'osi~\cite{WisemanDiosi2001}, yet the quantitative boundary between asymptotic exponential decay and finite-time attainment has rarely been isolated in closed form. Conversely, naive attempts to force finite-time hitting on pure states---such as imposing a Wright--Fisher martingale $dp_t = \sigma\sqrt{p_t(1-p_t)}\,dW_t$ directly on pure states---break linearity of the ensemble master equation and allow superluminal signaling via the Gisin--Hughston--Jozsa--Wootters (HJW) ensemble effect~\cite{Gisin1989,HJW1993}. Existing finite-time continuous pure-state collapse models evade this obstruction either via singular rates diverging at a preset deadline $T^*$ (Brody and Hughston~\cite{BrodyHughston2005,BrodyHughston2006}) or via non-Markovian colored noise~\cite{Mertens2021}.

This motivates two concrete questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item What is the quantitative speed limit preventing Markovian continuous pure-state unravelings of Born-preserving Gorini--Kossakowski--Sudarshan--Lindblad (GKSL) master equations from achieving zero-tail reduction in finite time?
\item Which sufficient relaxations of the theorem's hypotheses permit exact finite-time zero-tail collapse while reproducing an exact \emph{autonomous} Lindblad semigroup $\E[\rho_t] = e^{\mathcal{L}t}(\rho_0)$?
\end{enumerate}

Throughout, let $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}) := \{\rho \in \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}) : \rho = \rho^\dagger,\ \rho \ge 0,\ \Tr(\rho)=1\}$ denote the convex set of density matrices on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$. Section~\ref{sec:nogo} proves an arbitrary-dimension ($d\ge 2$) no-go theorem, universal log-tail floor, and $d=2$ global optimality corollary for homodyne CSL. Section~\ref{sec:exits} exhibits four sufficient escape hatches, analyzing both the unique \emph{Erlang-2 randomized Brody--Hughston} mixture and our \emph{Markovian continuous mixed-state collapse SDE} on $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^d \otimes \mathcal{H}_B)$. Section~\ref{sec:numerics} illustrates and cross-checks all analytical results from a single reproducible seeded Monte Carlo script, including unbiased signed signaling tests, multi-level ($d=3,4$) benchmarks, step-size convergence audits, and a damped-Rabi non-commuting Hamiltonian test. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} presents a calibrated assessment of physical meaning and limitations.

\section{Pure-State Continuous Zero-Tail No-Go Theorem}
\label{sec:nogo}

Let $\mathcal{H} = \mathbb{C}^d$ ($d \ge 2$) with orthonormal pointer basis $\{\ket{1},\dots,\ket{d}\}$ and projectors $P_k = \ketbra{k}{k}$. Consider a stochastic pure-state trajectory $\ket{\psi_t} = \sum_{j=1}^d \sqrt{p_j(t)} e^{i\phi_j(t)}\ket{j}$, with $\rho_t = \ketbra{\psi_t}{\psi_t} \in \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^d)$, populations $p_j(t) = \bra{j}\rho_t\ket{j} \ge 0$ ($\sum_{j=1}^d p_j(t)=1$), and coherences $\rho_{jk}(t) = \sqrt{p_j(t)p_k(t)}\,e^{i\theta_{jk}(t)}$ where $\theta_{jk}(t) = \phi_j(t) - \phi_k(t)$. Let $S_0 := \{m \in \{1,\dots,d\} : p_m(0) > 0\}$ be the initial support of $\ket{\psi_0}$.

\begin{theorem}[Pure-State Continuous Zero-Tail No-Go and Universal Log-Tail Floor]
\label{thm:nogo}
Suppose $\ket{\psi_t}$ evolves as a Markovian continuous It\^o semimartingale on $\mathbb{C}P^{d-1}$ driven by an $m$-dimensional standard Brownian motion $\vec{W}_t$ such that, for every starting time $s \ge 0$ and every initial pure state $\rho_s = \ketbra{\psi_s}{\psi_s}$, the ensemble average $\bar{\rho}_t = \E[\rho_t]$ ($t \ge s$) obeys a linear GKSL master equation $\dot{\bar{\rho}}_t = \mathcal{L}_t(\bar{\rho}_t)$ satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{Exact Born conservation:} $\bra{k}\mathcal{L}_t(\rho)\ket{k} = 0$ for all $\rho \in \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^d)$, all $k \in \{1,\dots,d\}$, and all $t \ge 0$;
\item[(ii)] \emph{Finite integrated dephasing:} for every finite $T < \infty$, $\int_0^T \Gamma_{\max}(t)\,dt < \infty$, where $\Gamma_{\max}(t) := \max_{j \ne k}\Gamma_{jk}(t)$ is the maximum pairwise dephasing rate of $\mathcal{L}_t$.
\end{enumerate}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Every Lindblad jump operator $L_\alpha(t)$ and Hamiltonian $H(t)$ in $\mathcal{L}_t$ is diagonal in $\{\ket{k}\}$, so $\bra{j}\mathcal{L}_t(\rho)\ket{k} = (-\Gamma_{jk}(t) - i\omega_{jk}(t))\rho_{jk}$ with $\Gamma_{jk}(t) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_\alpha |\ell_{\alpha,j}(t)-\ell_{\alpha,k}(t)|^2 \ge 0$.
\item[(b)] For any $m \notin S_0$, $p_m(t) = 0$ a.s.\ for all $t \ge 0$. On the open face $\{p_m > 0\ \forall m \in S_0\}$, $dp_j = \vec{\sigma}_j \cdot d\vec{W}_t$ (zero drift) and $d\theta_{jk} = \mu_{jk}\,dt + \vec{\eta}_{jk}\cdot d\vec{W}_t$, where the logarithmic volatilities $\vec{v}_j := \vec{\sigma}_j / p_j$ ($j \in S_0$) obey the exact pairwise identity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pairwise}
\frac{1}{8}\|\vec{v}_j - \vec{v}_k\|^2 + \frac{1}{2}\|\vec{\eta}_{jk}\|^2 = \Gamma_{jk}(t) \qquad (\forall j \ne k \in S_0),
\end{equation}
and the pointwise bound
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vbound}
\|\vec{v}_j\|^2 \le 8\Gamma_{\max}(t)\,(1 - p_j)^2.
\end{equation}
\item[(c)] \emph{Strict non-attainment (irreducible tails):} for every initially populated branch $j \in S_0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nonattain}
\Prob\bigl(p_j(t) > 0 \text{ for all } t \in [0,\infty)\bigr) = 1.
\end{equation}
\item[(d)] \emph{Universal log-tail floor:} for all $t \ge 0$ and all $j \in S_0$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:logfloor}
\E\!\left[\ln\frac{p_j(t)}{p_j(0)}\right] &\ge -4\int_0^t \Gamma_{\max}(s)\,\E\bigl[(1-p_j(s))^2\bigr]\,ds \nonumber\\
&\ge -4\bigl(1-p_j(0)\bigr)\int_0^t \Gamma_{\max}(s)\,ds,
\end{align}
where the second inequality is strict whenever $|S_0| \ge 2$ and $\int_0^t \Gamma_{\max}(s)\,ds > 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part (a).} Write $\mathcal{L}_t(\rho) = -i[H,\rho] + \sum_\alpha (L_\alpha\rho L_\alpha^\dagger - \frac{1}{2}\{L_\alpha^\dagger L_\alpha,\rho\})$. Evaluating condition~(i) on $\rho = \ketbra{m}{m}$ for $m \ne k$ gives $0 = \sum_\alpha |\bra{k}L_\alpha\ket{m}|^2$, hence every $L_\alpha$ is diagonal: $L_\alpha = \sum_k \ell_{\alpha,k}\ketbra{k}{k}$. Consequently, the dissipative term has zero diagonal on any off-diagonal operator, so evaluating condition~(i) on $\ketbra{k}{m}+\ketbra{m}{k}$ and $i(\ketbra{k}{m}-\ketbra{m}{k})$ forces $\bra{k}H\ket{m}=0$ for all $k\ne m$. Evaluating $\mathcal{L}_t$ on $\ketbra{j}{k}$ yields
\begin{align*}
\bra{j}\mathcal{L}_t(\ketbra{j}{k})\ket{k}
&= -i(E_j-E_k) \\
&\quad + \sum_\alpha\!\left(\ell_{\alpha,j}\ell_{\alpha,k}^* - \frac{|\ell_{\alpha,j}|^2 + |\ell_{\alpha,k}|^2}{2}\right),
\end{align*}
whose real part is $-\Gamma_{jk}(t) = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_\alpha |\ell_{\alpha,j}-\ell_{\alpha,k}|^2 \le 0$.

\emph{Part (b).} If $m \notin S_0$, then $p_m(t) \ge 0$ and $\E[p_m(t)] = p_m(0) = 0$ for all $t \ge 0$, so $p_m(t) = 0$ a.s.\ for all $t \ge 0$ by path continuity. If $|S_0| = 1$, then $p_j(t) \equiv 1$ and all claims hold trivially. Assume $|S_0| \ge 2$, fix $\epsilon \in (0, \min_{m \in S_0} p_m(0))$, and work up to the stopping time $\tau_\epsilon := \inf\{t \ge 0 : \min_{m \in S_0} p_m(t) \le \epsilon\}$. Because the diffusion is Markovian and satisfies the GKSL average when initialized at any time $s \ge 0$ from any pure state $\ket{\psi_s}$, the population drift vanishes identically: $\mu_j(\ket{\psi_s},s) = \frac{d}{dt}\E[p_j(t)]\big|_{t=s^+} = \bra{j}\mathcal{L}_s(\ketbra{\psi_s}{\psi_s})\ket{j} = 0$, giving $dp_j = \vec{\sigma}_j\cdot d\vec{W}_t$.

Applying It\^o's lemma to $\rho_{jk} = g(p_j,p_k)\,e^{i\theta_{jk}}$ ($j \ne k \in S_0$) with $g(x,y)=\sqrt{xy}$ ($g_x = \frac{g}{2x}$, $g_y = \frac{g}{2y}$, $g_{xx} = -\frac{g}{4x^2}$, $g_{yy} = -\frac{g}{4y^2}$, $g_{xy} = +\frac{g}{4xy}$) gives
\begin{align*}
\frac{dg}{g} &= \frac{1}{2}(\vec{v}_j+\vec{v}_k)\cdot d\vec{W}_t - \frac{1}{8}\|\vec{v}_j - \vec{v}_k\|^2\,dt, \\
\frac{d(e^{i\theta_{jk}})}{e^{i\theta_{jk}}} &= \left(i\mu_{jk} - \frac{1}{2}\|\vec{\eta}_{jk}\|^2\right)dt + i\vec{\eta}_{jk}\cdot d\vec{W}_t,
\end{align*}
while the cross quadratic variation $d\langle g, e^{i\theta_{jk}}\rangle / \rho_{jk} = \frac{i}{2}(\vec{v}_j+\vec{v}_k)\cdot\vec{\eta}_{jk}\,dt$ is purely imaginary. Equating the real part of the drift of $\rho_{jk}/\rho_{jk}$ at any face-interior pure state to $-\Gamma_{jk}(t)$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:pairwise}, which is the pure-dephasing specialization of the Wiseman--Di\'osi parameterization of diffusive unravelings~\cite{WisemanDiosi2001}.

Since $\sum_{k \in S_0} p_k(t) = 1$, we have $\sum_{k \in S_0} p_k \vec{v}_k = \sum_{k \in S_0} \vec{\sigma}_k = \vec{0}$, so $\vec{v}_j = \sum_{k \in S_0 \setminus \{j\}} p_k(\vec{v}_j - \vec{v}_k)$. By the triangle inequality and Eq.~\eqref{eq:pairwise} ($\|\vec{v}_j - \vec{v}_k\| \le \sqrt{8\Gamma_{jk}} \le \sqrt{8\Gamma_{\max}}$),
\begin{equation*}
\|\vec{v}_j\| \le \sum_{k \in S_0 \setminus \{j\}} p_k \|\vec{v}_j - \vec{v}_k\| \le \sqrt{8\Gamma_{\max}(t)}\,(1-p_j),
\end{equation*}
and squaring gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:vbound}.

\emph{Parts (c)--(d).} On $[0, \tau_\epsilon)$, It\^o's lemma on $\ln p_j(t)$ yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:logp_sde}
\ln\frac{p_j(t \wedge \tau_\epsilon)}{p_j(0)} = M_j(t \wedge \tau_\epsilon) - \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{t \wedge \tau_\epsilon} \|\vec{v}_j(s)\|^2\,ds,
\end{equation}
where $M_j(u) := \int_0^u \vec{v}_j(s)\cdot d\vec{W}_s$ is a continuous martingale with quadratic variation $\langle M_j\rangle_{T \wedge \tau_\epsilon} \le 8\int_0^T \Gamma_{\max}(s)\,ds < \infty$ uniformly in $\epsilon > 0$. By the Dambis--Dubins--Schwarz theorem~\cite{RevuzYor1999}, $M_j$ is a time-changed Brownian motion evaluated on a deterministically bounded clock interval $[0, 8\int_0^T\Gamma_{\max}(s)\,ds]$, so $\sup_{t \in [0,T]} |M_j(t \wedge \tau_\epsilon)|$ has Gaussian tails independent of $\epsilon$, while the drift in Eq.~\eqref{eq:logp_sde} is bounded below by $-4\int_0^T\Gamma_{\max}(s)\,ds$. Hence $\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\Prob(\tau_\epsilon \le T) = 0$ for every finite $T$, proving $\tau_0 := \lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\tau_\epsilon = \infty$ a.s., i.e., Eq.~\eqref{eq:nonattain}. Taking expectations in Eq.~\eqref{eq:logp_sde}, letting $\epsilon\downarrow 0$ (justified by $L^2$-boundedness of $M_j$ and boundedness of the drift), and substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:vbound} gives the first inequality in Eq.~\eqref{eq:logfloor}. Since $p_j(s)\in(0,1)$ a.s.\ when $|S_0|\ge 2$, we have $(1-p_j(s))^2 < 1-p_j(s)$ and $\E[(1-p_j(s))^2] < \E[1-p_j(s)] = 1-p_j(0)$, yielding the second (strict) inequality in Eq.~\eqref{eq:logfloor}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Pointwise Saturation and $d=2$ Global Optimality of Homodyne CSL]
\label{cor:csl}
For $d=2$ with constant dephasing rate $\Gamma > 0$ and $p_0 \in (0,1)$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Standard homodyne CSL ($dp_t^{\mathrm{CSL}} = \sqrt{8\Gamma}\,p_t^{\mathrm{CSL}}(1-p_t^{\mathrm{CSL}})\,dW_t$, $d\theta_t = 0$) satisfies $\|\vec{v}_1\|^2 = 8\Gamma(1-p_t^{\mathrm{CSL}})^2$, saturating Eq.~\eqref{eq:vbound} with equality and achieving $\E[\ln(p_t^{\mathrm{CSL}}/p_0)] = -4\Gamma\int_0^t \E[(1-p_s^{\mathrm{CSL}})^2]\,ds$.
\item[(b)] Among all $d=2$ Markovian continuous pure-state unravelings of $\Gamma$, homodyne CSL globally minimizes $\E[\ln p_t]$ at every $t \ge 0$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:csl_opt}
\E[\ln p_t] \ge \E[\ln p_t^{\mathrm{CSL}}] > \ln p_0 - 4(1-p_0)\Gamma t.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Part~(a) is immediate from Eq.~\eqref{eq:pairwise} with $\vec{\eta}_{12} = 0$. For Part~(b), any $d=2$ unraveling satisfying Eq.~\eqref{eq:pairwise} has $\|\vec{\sigma}_1\|^2 = 8\Gamma(1-\alpha_t)\,p_t^2(1-p_t)^2$ with adapted $\alpha_t := \|\vec{\eta}_{12}\|^2/(2\Gamma) \in [0,1]$. Define the continuous non-decreasing clock $A(t) := \int_0^t (1-\alpha_s)\,ds \le t$ and its right-continuous inverse $T(u) := \inf\{t \ge 0 : A(t) > u\}$. By the Dambis--Dubins--Schwarz theorem~\cite{RevuzYor1999}---extending the probability space with an independent Brownian motion beyond $A(\infty)$ whenever $A(\infty)<\infty$ so that the time-changed process is defined for all $u \ge 0$---the process $\tilde{p}_u := p_{T(u)}$ solves the homodyne CSL SDE $d\tilde{p}_u = \sqrt{8\Gamma}\tilde{p}_u(1-\tilde{p}_u)\,d\tilde{W}_u$ on $\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_u = \mathcal{F}_{T(u)}$, and $p_t = \tilde{p}_{A(t)}$ where $A(t) \le t$ is a bounded $\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_u$-stopping time (since $\{A(t)\le u\}=\{T(u)\ge t\}\in\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_u$). Because $\tilde{p}_u$ is a positive martingale and $x \mapsto \ln x$ is concave, $\ln \tilde{p}_u$ is a supermartingale bounded above by $0$ with finite expectation at $t$. By Doob's optional stopping theorem, $\E[\ln p_t] = \E[\ln \tilde{p}_{A(t)}] \ge \E[\ln \tilde{p}_t] = \E[\ln p_t^{\mathrm{CSL}}]$. The strict right-hand inequality follows because $(1-p_s^{\mathrm{CSL}})^2 < 1-p_s^{\mathrm{CSL}}$ on $(0,1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Sharpness for $d\ge 3$ and Scope]
Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} relies on five hypotheses: Markovianity, path continuity, pure-state trajectories, Born conservation~(i), and finite integrated dephasing~(ii). Hypothesis~(i) restricts the master equation to pure dephasing ($[H,P_k]=0$), excluding CSL with a non-commuting kinetic Hamiltonian. For $d \ge 3$, equality in the triangle-inequality bound~\eqref{eq:vbound} for a fixed branch $j$ requires all $\vec{v}_j - \vec{v}_k$ ($k \ne j$) to align with identical length $\sqrt{8\Gamma_{\max}}$ (so $\vec{v}_k = -\frac{p_j}{1-p_j}\vec{v}_j$ for all $k\ne j$, implying $\vec{v}_k - \vec{v}_l = \vec{0}$ for $k,l\ne j$). This cannot hold for all branches simultaneously. Moreover, in the Wiseman--Di\'osi representation $\vec{v}_a - \vec{v}_b = 2\,\mathrm{Re}(\vec{\ell}_a - \vec{\ell}_b)$ and $\vec{\eta}_{ab} = \mathrm{Im}(\vec{\ell}_a - \vec{\ell}_b)$ (where $\Gamma_{ab} = \frac{1}{2}\|\vec{\ell}_a-\vec{\ell}_b\|^2$), equality for branch $j$ is realized by the admissible single-channel homodyne unraveling of $L = \sqrt{2\Gamma}\,P_j$ (for which $\Gamma_{jk}=\Gamma$ and $\Gamma_{kl}=0$ for $k,l\ne j$), whereas for \emph{uniform} dephasing ($\Gamma_{ab}=\Gamma\ \forall a\ne b$) the vectors $\{\vec{\ell}_a\}$ form a regular $(d-1)$-simplex of edge $\sqrt{2\Gamma}$, making simultaneous alignment with $\|\mathrm{Re}(\vec{\ell}_j-\vec{\ell}_k)\|=\sqrt{2\Gamma}$ impossible for $d \ge 3$ (forcing $\mathrm{Im}(\vec{\ell}_k)=\mathrm{Im}(\vec{\ell}_j)$ and hence $\Gamma_{kl}=0$, a contradiction). Determining the tight optimal constant in Eq.~\eqref{eq:vbound} for uniform dephasing when $d \ge 3$ remains an interesting open geometric problem.
\end{remark}

\section{Sufficient No-Signaling Escape Hatches}
\label{sec:exits}

Keeping exact Born conservation~(i) and operational no-signaling fixed, we exhibit four sufficient escape hatches relaxing each of the remaining four hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} to achieve exact finite-time zero-tail collapse ($\Prob(\tau_{\mathrm{hit}} < \infty) = 1$):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Exit A (Drop Continuity):} Projective Poisson jumps ($\rho_t \to \ketbra{k}{k}$ with probability $p_k(0)$ at rate $\Gamma$) are Markovian, pure-state, obey $\E[\rho_t]=e^{\mathcal{L}t}(\rho_0)$, and collapse in mean time $\E[\tau] = 1/\Gamma$.
\item \textbf{Exit B (Drop Finite Integrated Dephasing):} Deterministic finite-time bridges of Brody--Hughston type~\cite{BrodyHughston2005,BrodyHughston2006} are obtained by time-changing homodyne CSL via $\tau(t) = -\ln(1 - t/T^*)$ on $[0, T^*)$, corresponding to the singular dephasing rate $\Gamma(t) = (T^*-t)^{-1}$ with $\int_0^{T^*}\Gamma(t)\,dt = \infty$, forcing continuous pure-state hitting at the preset deadline $T^*$.
\end{itemize}
We now analyze the two remaining relaxations, both of which reproduce the exact \emph{autonomous} Lindblad semigroup $e^{\mathcal{L}t}$.

\subsection{Exit C: Drop Markovianity (Randomized Brody--Hughston Mixture)}
\label{sec:exitC}

Sample a hidden, state-independent collapse deadline $T > 0$ once at $t=0$ from an Erlang-2 (Gamma$(2,\Gamma)$) density,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:erlang2}
f_T(s) = \Gamma^2 s\, e^{-\Gamma s} \qquad (s > 0), \qquad \E[T] = \frac{2}{\Gamma}.
\end{equation}
Conditional on $T$, evolve $\ket{\psi_t}$ on $[0, T)$ via the Exit~B bridge with singular rate $\Gamma_T(t) = (T-t)^{-1}$ (generated by $L_k(t) = \sqrt{\Gamma_T(t)}\,P_k$ for $d \ge 2$; for $d=2$, $dp_t = \sqrt{\frac{8}{T-t}}\,p_t(1-p_t)\,dW_t$), absorbing continuously at $\ketbra{k^*}{k^*}$ as $t \uparrow T$.

\begin{proposition}[Uniqueness of the Erlang-2 Deadline Density for Exit C]
Under the randomized Brody--Hughston bridge with deadline density $f_T$, every trajectory $t \mapsto \ket{\psi_t}$ is continuous, pure, and converges continuously to $\ket{k^*}$ at finite time $T<\infty$ with Born probability $p_{k^*}(0)$. Furthermore, the unconditional ensemble average satisfies the autonomous Lindblad semigroup
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exitC_lindblad}
\E_{T,W}[\rho_{jk}(t)] = \rho_{jk}(0)\, e^{-\Gamma t} \qquad (\forall t \ge 0,\ j \ne k)
\end{equation}
if and only if $f_T$ is the Erlang-2 density~\eqref{eq:erlang2}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under $\tau(t) = -\ln(1-t/T)$, the conditional process on $[0, T)$ is unit-rate homodyne CSL evaluated at $\tau(t)\to\infty$ as $t\uparrow T$, yielding conditional coherence $\E_W[\rho_{jk}(t)\mid T] = \rho_{jk}(0)(1-t/T)\mathbf{1}_{\{T>t\}}$. Averaging over $f_T$ equates Eq.~\eqref{eq:exitC_lindblad} to $\int_t^\infty f_T(s)(1 - t/s)\,ds = e^{-\Gamma t}$ for all $t \ge 0$. Differentiating once with respect to $t$ gives $-\int_t^\infty \frac{f_T(s)}{s}\,ds = -\Gamma e^{-\Gamma t}$, and differentiating a second time uniquely fixes $f_T(t) = \Gamma^2 t e^{-\Gamma t}$. Direct integration verifies the converse. Because $T$ is independent of the initial state, linearity extends to arbitrary bipartite states $\rho_{AB}(0)$, ensuring operational no-signaling.
\end{proof}

Exit~C is Exit~B averaged over random deadlines: conditional on $T$, the integrated rate still diverges, so hypothesis~(ii) is restored only at the ensemble level at the expense of Markovianity.

\subsection{Exit D: Drop Trajectory Purity (Markovian Mixed-State Continuous Collapse)}
\label{sec:exitD}

If we retain \emph{autonomous Markovian} dynamics on the quantum state space, \emph{continuous} paths, \emph{exact} finite-time zero-tail collapse, and linear Lindblad ensemble averages, Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} forces trajectories to leave the pure-state boundary and pass transiently through the interior of $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})$.

\subsubsection{Stage-Gated Markovian Mixed-State SDE (Exit D1)}
Let $\rho_{AB}(t) \in \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^d_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B)$ represent the global state of all degrees of freedom entangled with subsystem $A$, undergoing a pointer-commuting measurement ($[H, P_k]=0$) in Alice's basis $P_j = \ketbra{j}{j}_A \otimes I_B$. Decompose $\rho_{AB} = D + \rho_{\mathrm{off}}$ where $D = \sum_{j=1}^d P_j \rho_{AB} P_j = \sum_{j=1}^d \ketbra{j}{j}_A \otimes A_j$ ($A_j \ge 0$, $p_j = \Tr(A_j)$) and $\rho_{\mathrm{off}} = \sum_{j \ne k} P_j \rho_{AB} P_k$. Define the Markovian spectral coherence parameter
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mu_def}
\mu(\rho_{AB}) := -\lambda_{\min}\!\left(D^{-1/2}\rho_{\mathrm{off}} D^{-1/2}\right) \in [0, 1],
\end{equation}
using the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse on $\mathrm{supp}(D)$. We note three properties: (1)~$\rho_{AB} \ge 0 \iff \mu(\rho_{AB}) \le 1$, $\rho_{\mathrm{off}} = 0 \iff \mu(\rho_{AB}) = 0$, and any pure superposition has $\mu = 1$; (2)~for a single qubit ($d=2, d_B=1$), $\mu(\rho) = |\rho_{01}|/\sqrt{p(1-p)}$; (3)~on any uncorrelated product state $\rho_{AB} = \rho_A \otimes \rho_B$, $\mu(\rho_A\otimes\rho_B) = \mu(\rho_A)$ is strictly independent of $\rho_B$, whereas on an entangled state (e.g., $\ket{\Phi^+}$), $\mu(\rho_A)=0$ while $\mu(\rho_{AB})=1$, so the dynamics is fundamentally defined on the global density matrix.

With independent Brownian motions $W^{(1)}$ and antisymmetric $W^{(2)}_{jk} = -W^{(2)}_{kj}$ ($1 \le j < k \le d$), define the autonomous Markovian continuous SDE on $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^d_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B)$ by concatenating two stages at the stopping time $\tau_1 := \inf\{t \ge 0 : \mu_t = 0\}$:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:D1_off}
d\rho_{\mathrm{off}} &= -\Gamma \rho_{\mathrm{off}}\,dt + \sqrt{2\Gamma\mu_t(1-\mu_t)}\,\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{off}}}{\mu_t}\,dW_t^{(1)}, \\
\label{eq:D1_diag}
dA_j &= \sigma_p\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\mu_t = 0\}} \sum_{k \ne j} \sqrt{p_j p_k}\,\frac{A_j}{p_j}\,dW_{jk}^{(2)},
\end{align}
where $\sigma_p > 0$ is a free selection-rate parameter and $\rho_{\mathrm{off}}$ is absorbed at $0$ upon hitting $\mu_t = 0$. (In Phase~1, $\rho_{\mathrm{off}}(t)/\mu_t = \rho_{\mathrm{off}}(0)/\mu_0$ is a fixed bounded operator, avoiding any $0\cdot\infty$ indeterminacy at $\mu_t = 0$; the discontinuous indicator $\mathbf{1}_{\{\mu_t=0\}}$ simply marks concatenation of the Phase~2 diffusion onto Phase~1 at $\tau_1$.)

\begin{theorem}[Exact Properties of Exit D1]
\label{thm:exitD1}
For any initial state $\rho_{AB}(0) \in \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^d_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B)$ with $\mu_0 = \mu(\rho_{AB}(0)) \in (0, 1]$ and $p_j(0) = \Tr(A_j(0))$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \emph{Existence, uniqueness in law, continuity, and bipartite positivity:} Concatenating Eq.~\eqref{eq:D1_diag} onto Eq.~\eqref{eq:D1_off} at $\tau_1$ defines a continuous Markov process on $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^d_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B)$ that is unique in law (and pathwise unique for $d=2$) and satisfies $\rho_{AB}(t) \ge 0$ and $\Tr(\rho_{AB}(t)) = 1$ a.s.\ for all $t \ge 0$.
\item[(b)] \emph{Exact linear GKSL average and operational no-signaling:} $\E[\rho_{AB}(t)] = (e^{\mathcal{L}_A t} \otimes \mathcal{I}_B)(\rho_{AB}(0))$ identically for all $t \ge 0$ (a completely positive trace-preserving linear map on $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^d_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B)$), guaranteeing $\E[\rho_B(t)] = \rho_B(0)$ regardless of Alice's basis or $\sigma_p$.
\item[(c)] \emph{Finite-time zero-tail collapse and exact mean time:} Let $\tau_{\mathrm{tot}} = \inf\{t \ge 0 : \max_j p_j(t) = 1\}$. Then $\tau_{\mathrm{tot}} < \infty$ a.s., $\rho_{AB}(\tau_1) = D_0$ is the Lindblad decohered state, $\rho_{AB}(\tau_{\mathrm{tot}}) = \ketbra{k^*}{k^*}_A \otimes (A_{k^*}(0)/p_{k^*}(0))$ is the exact L\"uders post-measurement state with Born probability $\Prob(k^* = j) = p_j(0)$, and
\begin{align}
\label{eq:exact_tau}
\E[\tau_{\mathrm{tot}}] &= \frac{1}{\Gamma}\!\left(\frac{\pi^2}{6} - \Li_2(1-\mu_0)\right) \nonumber\\
&\quad - \frac{2}{\sigma_p^2}\sum_{j=1}^d (1-p_j(0))\ln(1-p_j(0)).
\end{align}
For any pure superposition ($\mu_0=1$) and any $d \ge 2$, $\E[\tau_{\mathrm{tot}}] \le \frac{\pi^2}{6\Gamma} + \frac{2}{\sigma_p^2}$ uniformly in $d$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Phase 1 ($t \in [0, \tau_1]$):} While $\mu_t > 0$, $dA_j = 0$, so $D_t = D_0$ is constant and $\rho_{\mathrm{off}}(t) = (\mu_t/\mu_0)\rho_{\mathrm{off}}(0)$, where $\mu_t$ satisfies the 1D Jacobi diffusion
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mu_sde}
d\mu_t = -\Gamma\mu_t\,dt + \sqrt{2\Gamma\mu_t(1-\mu_t)}\,dW_t^{(1)}.
\end{equation}
Since $\sqrt{2\Gamma\mu(1-\mu)}$ is $1/2$-H\"older continuous on $[0,1]$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:mu_sde} has a pathwise unique strong solution by the Yamada--Watanabe theorem~\cite{YamadaWatanabe1971}. Under $\mu_t = \sin^2(y_t/2)$ ($y_t \in [0,\pi]$), It\^o's lemma gives $dy_t = -\frac{\Gamma}{\sin y_t}\,dt + \sqrt{2\Gamma}\,dW_t^{(1)}$. Near $y=\pi$ ($\mu=1$), $w = \pi - y$ obeys $dw \approx \frac{\Gamma}{w}\,dt - \sqrt{2\Gamma}\,dW_t^{(1)}$, a 2D Bessel process ($\delta = 2$), for which $\mu=1$ is a Feller \emph{entrance} boundary~\cite{RevuzYor1999}: $\mu_t$ enters $(0,1)$ immediately and never exceeds $1$. On $\mathrm{supp}(D_0)$,
\begin{align*}
D_0^{-1/2}\rho_{AB}(t)D_0^{-1/2}
&= \Pi_{\mathrm{supp}(D_0)} + \frac{\mu_t}{\mu_0}D_0^{-1/2}\rho_{\mathrm{off}}(0)D_0^{-1/2} \\
&\ge (1-\mu_t)\Pi_{\mathrm{supp}(D_0)} \ge 0,
\end{align*}
establishing $\rho_{AB}(t) \ge 0$. Near $y=0$ ($\mu=0$), $dy_t \approx -\frac{\Gamma}{y_t}\,dt + \sqrt{2\Gamma}\,dW_t^{(1)}$ is a 0D Bessel process, so $\mu=0$ is an attainable \emph{exit} boundary hit in finite time $\tau_1 < \infty$ a.s. Moreover, $d(e^{\Gamma t}\mu_t) = e^{\Gamma t}\sqrt{2\Gamma\mu_t(1-\mu_t)}\,dW_t^{(1)}$ has bounded volatility on every finite $[0, T]$, making $e^{\Gamma t}\mu_t$ a true martingale; hence $\E[\mu_t] = \mu_0 e^{-\Gamma t}$ and $\E[\rho_{\mathrm{off}}(t)] = e^{-\Gamma t}\rho_{\mathrm{off}}(0)$ even after absorption at $\mu=0$. Solving the backward Kolmogorov ODE $\Gamma\mu(1-\mu)T_1'' - \Gamma\mu T_1' = -1$ ($T_1(0)=0$, $T_1'(1)$ finite) via $\frac{d}{d\mu}[(1-\mu)T_1'] = -(\Gamma\mu)^{-1}$ yields $T_1'(\mu) = -\frac{\ln\mu}{\Gamma(1-\mu)}$ and $T_1(\mu_0) = -\frac{1}{\Gamma}\int_0^{\mu_0}\frac{\ln x}{1-x}\,dx = \frac{1}{\Gamma}[\frac{\pi^2}{6} - \Li_2(1-\mu_0)]$.

\emph{Phase 2 ($t \ge \tau_1$):} Concatenating Eq.~\eqref{eq:D1_diag} at $\tau_1$ (where $\rho_{\mathrm{off}}\equiv 0$) gives $A_j(t) = p_j(t)\hat{A}_j$ with constant normalized blocks $\hat{A}_j = A_j(0)/p_j(0) \ge 0$ and $dp_j = \sigma_p \sum_{k \ne j}\sqrt{p_j p_k}\,dW_{jk}^{(2)}$. Antisymmetry $dW_{kj}^{(2)} = -dW_{jk}^{(2)}$ yields $\sum_j dp_j = 0$, $\E[dp_j]=0$, $d\langle p_j,p_j\rangle = \sigma_p^2 p_j(1-p_j)\,dt$, and $d\langle p_j,p_k\rangle = -\sigma_p^2 p_j p_k\,dt$ ($j \ne k$), which is the standard $d$-allele neutral Kimura/Wright--Fisher diffusion on the $(d-1)$-simplex~\cite{Kimura1955,Littler1975,Baxter2007}, uniquely determined in law for all $d \ge 2$~\cite{Ethier1976} (and pathwise unique for $d=2$ by Yamada--Watanabe~\cite{YamadaWatanabe1971}). Its generator $\mathcal{A} = \frac{\sigma_p^2}{2}\sum_j p_j(1-p_j)\partial_j^2 - \frac{\sigma_p^2}{2}\sum_{j\ne k} p_j p_k \partial_j\partial_k$ applied to $T_2(p) = -\frac{2}{\sigma_p^2}\sum_{j=1}^d (1-p_j)\ln(1-p_j)$ satisfies $\mathcal{A} T_2 = -\sum_j p_j = -1$ with $T_2=0$ at every pure vertex, establishing Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact_tau} and finite-time fixation onto a single vertex $k^*$ with martingale probability $\Prob(k^*=j) = p_j(0)$. Finally, $h(p) := p + (1-p)\ln(1-p)$ satisfies $h(0)=0$ and $h'(p) = -\ln(1-p) \ge 0$, so $-(1-p_j)\ln(1-p_j) \le p_j$, giving $\E[\tau_{\mathrm{tot}}-\tau_1] \le \frac{2}{\sigma_p^2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Physical Mechanism of Exit D1]
At $\tau_1$, \emph{every} trajectory sits at the exact same decohered state $D_0$, so Phase~1 carries no outcome information. Outcome selection occurs entirely in Phase~2 via a classical neutral Wright--Fisher process on the populations of $D_0$. Operational no-signaling holds because $\rho_0 \mapsto \E[\rho_t]$ is linear on $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})$ (whereas Gisin's argument~\cite{Gisin1989} assumes pure-state ontology). Conceptually, during $[0,\tau_{\mathrm{tot}})$ losing weights are relocated from pure-state amplitudes into classical mixed-state populations before hitting exact zero at $\tau_{\mathrm{tot}} < \infty$.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Two-Observer Commuting Extension on Extended State $(\rho_{AB},\mu_A,\mu_B)$}
\label{sec:two_obs}

Extending single-observer D1 to two simultaneous observers measuring $Z_A$ (rate $\Gamma_A$) and $B_\theta = \cos\theta\,Z_B + \sin\theta\,X_B$ (rate $\Gamma_B$) on $\ket{\Phi^+} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{00}+\ket{11})$ highlights a structural subtlety. In the commuting joint eigenbasis $\{\ket{0,b_0},\ket{0,b_1},\ket{1,b_0},\ket{1,b_1}\}$, evolve two independent local Jacobi decoherence factors $(\mu_A,\mu_B)$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:mu_sde} acting by Hadamard (Schur) multipliers $\rho_{AB}(t) = \rho_{AB}(0) \circ H_A(\mu_A) \circ H_B(\mu_B)$, where $H_A(\mu_A) = \begin{pmatrix}1 & \mu_A\\ \mu_A & 1\end{pmatrix}_A \otimes J_B \ge 0$ and $H_B(\mu_B) = J_A \otimes \begin{pmatrix}1 & \mu_B\\ \mu_B & 1\end{pmatrix}_B \ge 0$ (with $J$ the all-ones matrix), with Alice's Wright--Fisher block rescaling starting when $\mu_A=0$ and Bob's starting when $\mu_B=0$.

By the Schur product theorem, this dynamics preserves $\rho_{AB}(t) \ge 0$, reproduces $(e^{\mathcal{L}_A t}\otimes e^{\mathcal{L}_B t})(\rho_{AB}(0))$, satisfies two-way no-signaling, and makes every joint population $P_{ab}(t)$ a martingale ($dW_A dW_B = 0$) converging in finite time to a single joint outcome $(a^*,b^*)$ with exact joint L\"uders probabilities $|\braket{a,b_\theta}{\Phi^+}|^2$ (correlation $\langle Z_A B_\theta\rangle = \cos\theta$). \emph{However, this two-observer construction is not Markovian on $\rho_{AB}$ alone:} at $\theta=0$, the sole nonzero coherence of $\ket{\Phi^+}$ is $\rho_{00,11} = \frac{1}{2}\mu_A\mu_B$, so $(\mu_A,\mu_B)=(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ produce the identical diagonal matrix $\rho_{AB}$ yet different future evolutions. Thus, unlike single-observer Theorem~\ref{thm:exitD1}, this two-observer model lives on the \emph{extended state} $(\rho_{AB}, \mu_A, \mu_B)$.

\subsubsection{Smooth-Coupled Variant (Exit D2) and Non-Commuting Hamiltonians}
In Stage-Gated D1, if a persistent non-commuting Hamiltonian $H = \omega\sigma_x$ continuously regenerates off-diagonal coherence during a $Z$-basis measurement, the set $\{t : \mu_t = 0\}$ has Lebesgue measure zero, freezing Eq.~\eqref{eq:D1_diag}. To probe this regime on a single qubit ($\rho_t = \begin{pmatrix}p_t & z_t \\ z_t^* & 1-p_t\end{pmatrix}$, $r_t = |z_t|$, $u_t := p_t(1-p_t)-r_t^2 = \det\rho_t$), adding $-i[\omega\sigma_x,\rho_t]\,dt$ ($\dot{p} = -2\omega\,\mathrm{Im}\,z$, $\dot{z} = +i\omega(2p-1)$) yields the smooth-coupled Bloch-ball SDE (Exit~D2):
\begin{align}
\label{eq:D2_z}
dz_t &= \bigl(+i\omega(2p_t-1) - \Gamma z_t\bigr)\,dt + \sqrt{2\Gamma\frac{u_t}{r_t}}\,z_t\,dW_t^{(1)}, \\
\label{eq:D2_p}
dp_t &= -2\omega\,\mathrm{Im}(z_t)\,dt + \sigma_p \frac{u_t}{\sqrt{p_t(1-p_t)}}\,dW_t^{(2)},
\end{align}
with radial noise vanishing as $\sqrt{2\Gamma r_t u_t} \to 0$ when $r_t \to 0$. Unitary evolution preserves $\det\rho_t = u_t$, while near $u_t \to 0^+$ with $r_t > 0$, It\^o's lemma gives $du_t = [2\Gamma r_t^2 - O(u_t)]\,dt - 2\sqrt{2\Gamma}\,r_t^{3/2}\sqrt{u_t}\,dW_t^{(1)} + O(u_t)\,dW_t^{(2)}$, whose Feller entrance condition $2(2\Gamma r_t^2) \ge 8\Gamma r_t^3 \iff r_t \le \frac{1}{2}$ holds uniformly on the Bloch ball. Because the drift of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:D2_z}--\eqref{eq:D2_p} is linear in $(p_t, z_t)$ and the noise has zero mean and bounded variance, the exact ensemble averages $(\E[p_t], \E[z_t])$ automatically obey the damped-Rabi Lindblad ODE. For $\omega=0$, we also consider a \emph{normalized} smooth-coupled variant (D2-norm) in which the radial diffusion coefficient matches the Jacobi scaling of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mu_sde}:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:D2_norm_r}
dr_t &= -\Gamma r_t\,dt + \sqrt{2\Gamma r_t\bigl(\sqrt{p_t(1-p_t)} - r_t\bigr)}\,dW_t^{(1)}, \\
\label{eq:D2_norm_p}
dp_t &= \sigma_p\!\left(1 - \frac{r_t^2}{p_t(1-p_t)}\right)\!\sqrt{p_t(1-p_t)}\,dW_t^{(2)}.
\end{align}
As shown in Section~\ref{sec:numerics}, however, exact finite-time hitting for both Eqs.~\eqref{eq:D2_z}--\eqref{eq:D2_p} and Eqs.~\eqref{eq:D2_norm_r}--\eqref{eq:D2_norm_p} remains analytically open due to 2D corner dynamics near $(p=0, r=0)$.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} and Theorem~\ref{thm:exitD1} isolate a crisp structural boundary for pure-dephasing objective collapse models. If one demands autonomous Markovian dynamics on the quantum state space, continuous paths, exact finite-time zero-tail collapse, Born conservation, and linear Lindblad ensemble averages, individual trajectories cannot remain on the pure-state manifold $\mathbb{C}P^{d-1}$: they must pass transiently through mixed density matrices $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})$. This offers a concrete dynamical perspective on \emph{Density-Matrix Realism}~\cite{Chen2019}.

Four limitations must be emphasized transparently:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Operational indistinguishability on ensemble averages:} Because CSL, Exit~A, Exit~C, and Exit~D1 all unravel the identical single-time Lindblad semigroup $e^{\mathcal{L}t}$, no standard ensemble tomography measuring $\Tr(O\bar{\rho}_t)$ can distinguish them. They differ only in nonlinear trajectory moments (such as $\E[p_t^2]$), which could become observable only if an external classical or gravitational field coupled directly to individual ontic states.
\item \emph{Global state dependence and multi-observer scaling:} Single-observer Exit~D1 is Markovian only on the \emph{global} density matrix of all entangled subsystems (since $\mu(\rho_A)=0 \ne \mu(\rho_{AB})=1$ on entangled states). Our simultaneous two-observer construction requires the extended state $(\rho_{AB},\mu_A,\mu_B)$ rather than $\rho_{AB}$ alone. Likewise, because $\sigma_p$ is an unconstrained free parameter, macroscopic amplification ($\E[\tau_{\mathrm{tot}}]\propto 1/N$ for an $N$-particle pointer) is not derived: it would require prescribing $\sigma_{p,N}^2 \propto N$ and resolving the multi-observer Markovian formulation on $\rho$ alone.
\item \emph{Physical status of tails in Exit D1:} During $[0, \tau_{\mathrm{tot}})$, losing weights are relocated from pure-state amplitudes into classical mixed-state populations of $D_0$ before hitting exact zero at $\tau_{\mathrm{tot}} < \infty$.
\item \emph{Non-commuting Hamiltonians and open problems:} Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} and Theorem~\ref{thm:exitD1} assume pure dephasing ($[H,P_k]=0$). Under persistent $[H,P_k]\ne 0$, D1's indicator $\mathbf{1}_{\{\mu_t=0\}}$ stalls, while exact finite-time hitting for smooth-coupled D2 remains open. Determining the tight speed-limit constant in Eq.~\eqref{eq:vbound} for uniform dephasing when $d \ge 3$ and constructing a strictly Markovian multi-observer mixed-state collapse SDE under non-commuting Hamiltonians are left for future work.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Numerical Discretization Schemes for Exit D2}
\label{app:d2_numerics}

To ensure that every numerical figure in Section~\ref{sec:numerics} is tied to an explicit discrete scheme, we specify the two discretizations used for Exit~D2.

\emph{1. Normalized D2 in Table~\ref{tab:dt_conv} ($\omega=0$).}
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:D2_norm_r}--\eqref{eq:D2_norm_p} are integrated on a step $dt$ with independent increments $\Delta W_1, \Delta W_2 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, dt)$. Writing $q = p(1-p)$ and $b(p) = \sigma_p(\sqrt{q} - r^2/\sqrt{q})$, holding $r$ fixed gives $\frac{1}{2}b(p)b'(p) = \frac{\sigma_p^2}{4}(1-\mu^4)(1-2p)$ with $\mu = r/\sqrt{q}$. The Milstein update on $p_n \in (0,1)$ is therefore
\begin{align*}
p_{n+1} &= \mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\Bigl[p_n + \sigma_p(1-\mu_n^2)\sqrt{p_n(1-p_n)}\,\Delta W_2 \\
&\qquad\qquad\quad + \frac{\sigma_p^2}{4}(1-\mu_n^4)(1-2p_n)(\Delta W_2^2 - dt)\Bigr],
\end{align*}
followed (for $r_n > 0$) by a Milstein update on $r_n$ with $r_{\max,n} = \sqrt{p_n(1-p_n)}$ and $r_{\max,n+1} = \sqrt{p_{n+1}(1-p_{n+1})}$,
\begin{align*}
\tilde{r}_{n+1} &= r_n - \Gamma r_n\,dt + \sqrt{2\Gamma r_n(r_{\max,n}-r_n)}\,\Delta W_1 \\
&\quad + \frac{\Gamma}{2}(r_{\max,n} - 2r_n)(\Delta W_1^2 - dt),
\end{align*}
with reflection $\tilde{r}_{n+1} \mapsto 2r_{\max,n+1} - \tilde{r}_{n+1}$ if $\tilde{r}_{n+1} > r_{\max,n+1}$ and absorption at $0$. Table~\ref{tab:six_models} (row D2) uses a plain Euler update for $p_n$ paired with the Milstein update for $r_n$ on Eqs.~\eqref{eq:D2_z}--\eqref{eq:D2_p} at $\omega=0$ with $u_n = p_n(1-p_n)-r_n^2$.

\emph{2. Symmetric positivity-preserving martingale integrator for $H = \omega\sigma_x$.}
Under $H = \omega\sigma_x$ starting from $\ket{0}$, naive upper-boundary reflection on $r_n$ introduces a strong downward bias whenever $p_{n+1}$ fluctuates toward $1$ and shrinks $r_{\max,n+1}$. We eliminate this bias via a three-step operator splitting over $dt$ (with $z_n = x_n + i y_n$, $s_{z,n} = 2p_n - 1$):
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item \emph{Exact unitary rotation:}
\begin{align*}
s_z' &= s_{z,n}\cos(2\omega dt) - 2y_n\sin(2\omega dt), \\
y' &= y_n\cos(2\omega dt) + \frac{1}{2}s_{z,n}\sin(2\omega dt),
\end{align*}
with $p' = \frac{1}{2}(1+s_z')$, $x'=x_n$, and $r' = \sqrt{(x')^2 + (y')^2}$, preserving $u' = p'(1-p')-(r')^2 \ge 0$ identically.
\item \emph{Symmetric truncated martingale step for $p$:} Let $p_{\mathrm{lo,hi}} = \frac{1}{2} \mp \sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-(r')^2}$ and $m_p = \min(p'-p_{\mathrm{lo}}, p_{\mathrm{hi}}-p') \ge 0$. Set
\begin{equation*}
p_{n+1} = p' + \mathrm{clip}_{[-m_p, m_p]}\!\left(\sigma_p \frac{u'}{\sqrt{p'(1-p')}}\,\Delta W_2\right).
\end{equation*}
Because $\Delta W_2$ is symmetrically distributed around $0$, $\E[p_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_n] = p'$ identically and $p_{n+1}(1-p_{n+1}) \ge (r')^2$.
\item \emph{Symmetric truncated radial step for $z$:} Let $r_{\max} = \sqrt{p_{n+1}(1-p_{n+1})}$, $r_{\mathrm{det}} = r' e^{-\Gamma dt} \le r_{\max}$, and $m_r = \min(r_{\mathrm{det}}, r_{\max}-r_{\mathrm{det}}) \ge 0$. Set
\begin{equation*}
r_{n+1} = r_{\mathrm{det}} + \mathrm{clip}_{[-m_r, m_r]}\!\left(\sqrt{2\Gamma r_{\mathrm{det}}(r_{\max}^2 - r_{\mathrm{det}}^2)}\,\Delta W_1\right),
\end{equation*}
and rescale $(x_{n+1}, y_{n+1}) = (r_{n+1}/r')(x', y')$ for $r' > 0$. By symmetry of $\Delta W_1$, $\E[z_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_n] = z' e^{-\Gamma dt}$ identically and $0 \le r_{n+1} \le r_{\max}$, guaranteeing $\rho_{n+1} \ge 0$ with zero boundary-reflection bias (leaving only standard $O(dt)$ Lie--Trotter splitting error).
\end{enumerate}
